# ADR-020 — ADM-00 Admin Platform architecture and governance

**Status:** OWNER APPROVED at the ADM-00 product-policy level, 2026-11-10. Only items labelled **OWNER APPROVED** are owner decisions. Detailed role defaults beyond those decisions remain **PROPOSED**; implementation-specific security design and other gated choices remain **OPEN**. This record extends [ADR-008](architecture-decisions.md#adr-008--git-managed-markdown-editorial-content-accepted) without replacing its current Git-managed News delivery. It is the ADM-00 decision record for the [central roadmap](azerdex-product-roadmap.md) and [access-control specification](admin-platform-access-control.md).

## Context, goals and boundary

AzerDex needs a manually operated `/admin` News CMS in which staff can draft, review and publish content without a code commit or Vercel deployment. Existing Supabase Auth supplies identity, not staff authority. The private account/Companion/Guildbook database, the public read database and its publication worker, and the website release process are distinct trust domains. The CMS must keep drafts and unapproved media private and expose only an authorized published revision and approved assets. The ADM-00 goal is a reviewable authorization and editorial contract before ADM-01, while leaving storage and delivery architecture to explicit later gates.

## Owner-approved policy decisions

The following are **OWNER APPROVED** product and governance rules; no implementation design gains approval by association:

1. The five system roles are **User, Moderator, Editor, Administrator and Owner**. An account may hold multiple roles and controlled individual permission grants. Group management UI is deferred beyond V1.
2. An Editor has no publishing right by default. An explicit, scoped `news.article.publish` grant may delegate it. A delegated Editor may publish their own validated **normal** article. An Administrator also needs separate publishing authorization; the Administrator role alone does not confer it. An Owner may publish a validated normal article.
3. A highlighted or otherwise high-impact article needs independent approval by another authorized person. `news.article.approve_highlighted` belongs to an Administrator or Owner, or to an Editor with a specific approval grant. Its author or substantive editor cannot approve that revision, including when holding Owner. An Owner may publish highlighted content only after another eligible person approves it.
4. Approval binds to one immutable article revision and is invalid if that revision changes. Classification cannot be downgraded merely to evade mandatory approval.
5. Delegated publishing grants have a **30-day default and 90-day maximum**, expire automatically and are audited. Renewal is a new reviewed grant.
6. Staff MFA requires **TOTP at AAL2**. Critical actions require fresh authentication no older than **15 minutes**; the exact enforcement and recovery design needs security review.
7. The initial Owner bootstrap is one-time and audited; the last active Owner is protected, and recovery is controlled. The first-Owner identity and the bootstrap/recovery mechanics are not approved by this policy decision.
8. Audit retention has a **365-day baseline**, subject to privacy/security review of implementation, exceptions and deletion rules.
9. Authorization is server-side, default-deny and checked again for revocation at the next protected decision. Application roles and editorial publishing give no automatic production, database/public-read publication, deployment or other privileged operational authority.

## Proposed authorization contract

**OWNER APPROVED model:** the five roles, multiple roles per account and controlled individual grants in the section above. **PROPOSED implementation:** role-to-permission mappings are versioned and changed through reviewed policy updates. Optional group-based role inheritance is a later extension; group management UI is beyond V1. If group inheritance is added, it must use the same central evaluation path. No role name, frontend menu, editable user metadata or JWT claim alone authorizes an action.

**OWNER APPROVED invariants:** server-side effective-permission evaluation, default deny, next-decision revocation, controlled grants and separation from production privileges. **PROPOSED enforcement design:** evaluate at protected entry and immediately before each side effect; combine active role mappings and grants for the authenticated account, intersect resource, environment and time scope, then apply suspension, revocation, MFA and separation-of-duties rules. Unknown permission, missing policy state or failed check denies access. Any cache must preserve next-decision revocation, including for an open session. No unrestricted permission wildcard exists. A grantor cannot delegate beyond an approved ceiling, approve their own elevation or use a second role to bypass an action gate.

Permissions below preserve the proposed stable names. `admin.enter` is necessary for staff entry, never sufficient for an action. `news.article.publish` is an editorial permission only. `ops.status.read` is read-only and scoped to explicitly approved status data. Publishing and highlighted-approval cells implement the **OWNER APPROVED** rules; other role defaults are **PROPOSED** for security/technical review. `G` means eligible only through an individually approved scoped grant; `—` means no default role permission. An approved grant still requires the action-specific checks.

The matrix does not itself approve assignments. `G` requires an explicit grant even when the person has that role; in particular, the Administrator role never grants `news.article.publish` by itself. Owner `news.article.publish` covers validated normal articles and highlighted articles only after independent approval. Approval permissions cannot authorize one's own revision or staff elevation. `news.article.review` records review and cannot substitute for `news.article.approve_highlighted`. Permission scopes include article or collection, action, environment, start and expiry as applicable. Every direct grant records subject, permission, scope, reason/ticket, proposer, approver, effective time, expiry and revocation. **OWNER APPROVED:** delegated `news.article.publish` grants default to 30 days and may last no longer than 90 days; expiry and use are audited. Renewal is a new reviewed grant.

`production.deploy`, database/public-read publication, SQL mutation, Cron/secret changes and other privileged operations are **outside** this CMS matrix. They require their existing separate identities, approvals, protected environments and release gates. A status panel or an Owner role does not imply those capabilities.

## Editorial state machine and publication rules

**PROPOSED state names and transitions:** `Draft → In Review → Approved → Published → Archived / Unpublished`. A review can return a revision to Draft; an approved revision can be returned or superseded; unpublishing/archiving can retain a private revision history. For a normal article, Approved records completed validation and may be entered by its authorized publisher; it does not assert a second person's review. An extra normal-article review may be added by later editorial policy without removing the approved self-publishing authority. For highlighted/high-impact content, Approved requires the independent revision-bound approval. Each content change creates a new immutable revision ID. The workflow must record article ID, revision ID, classification, actor and transition. State alone never grants permission; every transition checks the action permission and current revision.

| Path | Required actions and checks |
| --- | --- |
| Normal article | Author drafts and previews, validates content and approved media, then submits. A delegated Editor with an active scoped `news.article.publish` grant may publish their own validated normal revision. An Administrator needs a separate publishing grant; an Owner may publish. All require the same validation. A second-person review is not required by the owner-approved normal-article policy. |
| Highlighted / high-impact article | Author drafts and submits an immutable revision. Another person with `news.article.approve_highlighted` reviews that exact revision, validates its preview/media and records an approval. That approver cannot be its author or substantive editor, including when they hold Owner. An authorized publisher may publish only the unchanged approved revision after rechecking the approval and current classification. If no independent eligible approver exists, publication waits. |
| Change after approval | Any content, metadata, media, classification or SEO change creates a new revision and invalidates the earlier revision's approval for publication. A changed revision returns to the applicable review path. |
| Classification downgrade | Classification is tracked with revision history. A downgrade cannot erase a prior highlighted/high-impact requirement to circumvent review; it needs a separately authorized, reasoned classification decision, recorded in audit, under a policy set before ADM-03. Until then, the stricter approval requirement applies. |
| Unpublish / rollback | A separately authorized `news.article.unpublish` action removes the public revision and updates dependent outputs. Rollback selects a previously validated revision through the current approval rules; a past approval is not automatically reusable after intervening changes. Both actions are audited and testable. |

**PROPOSED delivery invariants:** preview resolves only private drafts and approved media for authorized staff, with no public caching or indexing. Media provenance, rights, alt text, type and size checks precede publish. The public article, RSS, sitemap, canonical URL and SEO metadata must move coherently to the same published revision, including on unpublish and rollback. No draft, unapproved asset, private account record or Guildbook datum may leak through public pages, feeds, caches or the public read model. Publishing content must work without a code commit or Vercel deployment. The storage, consistency/atomicity and cache invalidation design is **OPEN** for ADM-03; this ADR chooses no CMS database, asset store, publication job or endpoint.

Audit events must cover draft creation and revision, classification change, review/approval/rejection, validation result, grant use, publish, unpublish, rollback and denied attempts. Each records actor, time, article/revision, action, outcome, reason and relevant grant/approval identifiers without storing secrets or private article payloads in the audit trail.

## Security and staff administration

**OWNER APPROVED security policy:** staff MFA uses TOTP/AAL2 and critical actions need authentication no older than 15 minutes. Critical actions include publish/unpublish, highlighted approval, staff assignment, grant approval and recovery. Authorization is server-side, default-deny and checks revocation at the next protected decision. **OPEN implementation design:** how to verify AAL2 and freshness against existing Supabase Auth sessions, safely recover a lost factor and enforce revocation without stale claims. A verified, active, non-anonymous account is the proposed staff prerequisite. Staff authorization state is private and server-controlled. A stale JWT, user-editable metadata, `authenticated` database role or hidden menu is insufficient. Private staff data and draft/media storage need separately reviewed access boundaries, with no privileged key in the browser. Supabase exposed schema grants/RLS and any privileged database functions need ADM-01/03 review if used. The public read database continues to contain only its approved aggregate allowlist, never staff state or Guildbook data.

**OWNER APPROVED boundaries:** one-time audited bootstrap, controlled recovery, last-Owner protection and next-decision revocation. **PROPOSED lifecycle mechanics:** role and grant requests record requester, target, scope, reason and expiry. An authorized approver checks the proposed ceiling and conflicts, uses step-up authentication, and records an immutable decision. Administrator/Owner assignment and elevated grants require independent eligible approval; subject and proposer cannot approve their own elevation. Suspension and revocation are audited and trigger session invalidation when warranted. Periodic access review removes stale assignments. The last active Owner cannot be removed or disabled through ordinary administration before a safe successor exists.

**PROPOSED bootstrap/recovery mechanics:** an operator selects an approved existing verified account by exact ID, verifies fresh authentication and MFA, assigns the initial Owner once, writes a durable audit event and disables the bootstrap path. It is never public registration, automatic first-user promotion, hard-coded email or reusable token. A single Owner cannot provide two distinct approvers initially, so a documented, MFA-gated initial assignment ceremony with reason, out-of-band notice and retrospective review is proposed until a second eligible approver exists. Subsequent protected assignments use independent approval. Lost-owner recovery needs a separate short-lived, audited procedure and cannot bypass last-Owner protection silently. The operator controls and ceremony design remain **OPEN** for review before their isolated implementation slice; the actual first-Owner identity, operational credentials and recovery runbook require separate approval before production use.

**OWNER APPROVED retention baseline:** 365 days, subject to privacy/security review. **PROPOSED audit mechanics:** capture staff authentication/step-up outcomes, authorization denials, role/grant proposals, approvals, expiry and revocations, bootstrap/recovery, editorial events and moderation decisions. Protect against alteration, limit audit readers, and avoid credentials and sensitive payloads. Storage, export, exceptions, deletion and incident access rules remain **OPEN** for privacy/security review.

## Scope, consequences and acceptance

This ADR documents ADM-00 only. It does not create production schema, secrets, roles, privileged endpoints, CMS delivery architecture or a deployment; it does not implement ADM-01 or change `release`. The hybrid model gives narrow delegation and multiple-role flexibility, at the cost of versioned policy administration, per-action server checks, stronger audit and careful revocation. Independent highlighted approval may delay publication when staffing is thin. Deployment-independent publishing requires a later content-delivery design and explicit consistency tests.

ADM-00 is **accepted at the product-policy level** with this owner approval and consistent ADR, specification and roadmap. Technical/security acceptance remains gated. ADM-01 may proceed with isolated, non-production implementation slices after their relevant design reviews under the [ADM-01A gate sequence](adm-01a-security-architecture-plan.md#9-decision-register-and-four-distinct-gates). The later implementation-review, Admin MFA staging-acceptance and production-activation gates remain separate; no real Owner role or privileged production functionality is activated until the required security and first-Owner approvals. ADM-02 builds private News drafting/media; ADM-03 designs and implements editorial delivery; ADM-04 adds moderation/read-only operations; ADM-05 validates staged rollout. Each phase has separate acceptance evidence and production GO in the [roadmap](azerdex-product-roadmap.md#centralized-admin-platform-adm-00adm-05).
