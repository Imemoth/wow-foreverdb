## 2. Product policy versus proposed mechanics

**OWNER APPROVED in ADR-020:** Editor publishing needs an explicit scoped grant; a delegated Editor may publish their own validated normal revision. Administrator needs a separate publish grant; Owner may publish normal content. Highlighted/high-impact content requires approval of the exact unchanged revision by an independent Administrator/Owner, or specifically granted Editor. The author and any substantive editor cannot approve it, including when Owner. Classification downgrade cannot evade that approval. `news.article.unpublish` is separate. Publishing grants default to 30 days, cap at 90 days; staff need TOTP/AAL2 and critical actions need server-authoritative freshness within 15 minutes. These decisions are not reopened here.

**OWNER APPROVED for ADM-03A:** stable article IDs and immutable revision snapshots; independent, revision-bound approvals; a private CMS source of truth alongside the Admin/Auth authorization database; protected action, audit and outbox writes in one private transaction; a dedicated public-read projection and delivery pipeline; no classification downgrade in V1; preservation without automatic migration of Git-managed articles and their URLs; complete News Collector isolation and identity/URL/RSS compatibility; and V1 text plus images with verified usage rights, provenance and attribution. These are architecture decisions, not approval of a particular schema, worker, hosted environment or measured cross-database risk.

**NOT APPROVED:** any maximum publication-versus-revocation exposure window across databases, production publication activation, legacy Git-content cutover, hosted Admin/CMS migrations or production delivery-worker activation. A target-configured staging measurement, security/data review and separate owner risk acceptance are required for the exposure window. The private transaction cannot make revocation and public delivery atomic across databases.

**TECHNICAL PROPOSALS below:** exact snapshot serialization, table/constraint layout, state decomposition, implementation of the classification floor and single-use approval, concurrency lock order, outbox dispatch/compensation and cache invalidation. Role defaults beyond ADR-020's approved cells remain subject to existing security review. An active permission and a matching approval are necessary but never sufficient without content, rights and delivery validation.

## 3. Stable identity and immutable snapshots

1. A manually authored article receives a random, permanent `article_id` (UUID) and one globally unique **manual-News** slug. The slug is reserved and immutable from creation in V1; `/news/{slug}` is its canonical URL once published. Reserve route segments such as `entry` and `rss.xml`. Do not use a title, mutable slug or upstream Collector ID as an authorization identity. Article and revision IDs never change on edit, unpublish or republish.
2. `origin` is `manual` or `legacy_git`. Collector rows remain in `pub.news_entries`, outside this table. A `legacy_git` mapping records the exact path, slug and imported bytes/hash if an import is later approved. Display author text is not an Auth user ID. No automatic conversion of a Git article into an active staff-owned CMS article is allowed.
3. Every public-output-affecting edit creates a new `revision_id` and monotonic per-article revision number. The revision snapshot is immutable after insert: title, summary, Markdown/body or reviewed blocks, byline, category, tags, classification, featured flag, sample flag, SEO/canonical metadata, approved media references with immutable asset versions and rights/alt text, related links, and validation schema version. Normalize and hash the complete canonical snapshot server-side. A client-supplied hash is never authority. Rendered HTML is derived by the reviewed renderer, never accepted as executable client markup.
4. The database records `created_by`, creation time, parent revision, and a server-derived set of author/substantive-editor identities from the edit history. **Fail-closed V1 rule:** every change to content or public metadata counts as substantive for independence; an editorial owner may later approve narrower exceptions. Editing an already published article starts a new working revision while the old published revision remains live.
5. `current_revision_id` is the working head; `published_revision_id` is the live target last acknowledged by the public read model. They are distinct. Revision review state (`draft`, `in_review`, `approved`, `rejected`, `superseded`) is distinct from delivery state (`never_published`, `publish_pending`, `published`, `unpublish_pending`, `unpublished`, `delivery_failed`). Thus a live article can have a newer draft without exposing it or treating it as published.

## 4. Revision-bound approval and publication

- A review request identifies `article_id`, `revision_id`, server-computed snapshot hash and classification floor. An edit makes a new head revision; it cannot mutate the submitted snapshot. Review and approval of an old revision remain historical evidence but cannot authorize the new head.
- Approval is an append-only decision bound to article, revision, hash, effective classification, approver, timestamp, policy version and any individual grant. `news.article.review` may record ordinary feedback; it cannot stand in for `news.article.approve_highlighted`.
- A highlighted/high-impact approval must be by another currently eligible person. Recheck approver independence against all server-recorded authors and substantive editors, plus current staff status, role/grant, policy version and expiry at **publication**, not merely at approval time. A person holding multiple roles cannot combine them to bypass independence.
- One approval authorizes at most one publication intent. A unique consumption record keyed by `approval_id` and a new idempotent `publication_intent_id` prevent double publish and replay. Unpublish, rollback or a later edit requires fresh validation and a new approval for highlighted/high-impact content, even when selecting an old byte-identical revision. Normal revisions require the same validation but no second person solely because they are normal.
- Treat `featured=true` as at least highlighted for manual content. Track the highest classification required by the current head and any previous high-impact classification in a protected `classification_floor`. V1 does **not** lower that floor through an ordinary edit or remove a highlighted approval requirement. A future downgrade path needs a separately authorized, reasoned and audited decision by an independent eligible classifier; until that design is approved, the stricter rule wins. Collector `featured` remains a source-card curation signal, not a CMS approval.
- Validate allowed content structure, preview, safe links, approved media versions and rights/provenance before approval and again before publication. A media replacement or changed alt text creates a new revision. The public projection receives only validated bytes and approved assets.

## 5. State transitions

State labels are per working revision or delivery pointer as indicated. A denial leaves both unchanged and may be audited without creating a success event.

| From → to | Guard and durable effect | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| None → Draft r1 | Reserve slug/article ID; create immutable r1, author/edit event and audit. Private only. | `news.draft.write` + `admin.enter` |
| Draft/Rejected/Approved rN → Draft rN+1 | New snapshot/number/head, all changed fields and editors recorded; prior approval cannot follow the new head. Live pointer, if any, stays on rN or older. | `news.draft.write` scoped to article |
| Draft → In Review | Lock article/head, validate and submit exact hash; record request. | `news.article.submit` |
| In Review → Draft/Rejected | Reasoned review/return; no public effect. | `news.article.review` under proposed role default |
| In Review → Approved | Normal: validation state, possibly entered by authorized publisher. Highlighted: append independent exact-revision decision after validation and fresh MFA. | `news.article.approve_highlighted` for highlighted; normal validation path never implies this permission |
| Approved → Publish pending | Recheck current head/hash, classification floor, rights/media, live permission/grant and any required independent unused approval; consume that approval when required, append success audit and durable outbox in the same private transaction. | `news.article.publish`; Owner default normal, Editor/Administrator explicit scoped grant |
| Publish pending → Published | Public-read transaction accepts only the next article generation and atomically switches its public pointer; receipt confirms delivery. Until receipt, old public revision stays live. | Dedicated narrow delivery worker, **no staff privilege** |
| Published → Unpublish pending → Unpublished | `news.article.unpublish` intent/audit first; public-read pointer removal and feed/sitemap exclusion on receipt. Retain private history and tombstone. | Separately authorized staff action, then worker |
| Published/Unpublished → Draft rN+1 → Publish pending / rollback | Copy the selected historical snapshot into a **new head revision**, retaining its provenance; rerun current validation, grant and classification-floor checks. Do not reuse an old approval. | `news.draft.write`, current `news.article.publish` and fresh independent approval when required |

`Approved` never means public. Delivery failure leaves the previous public pointer visible, marks the intent failed/retryable and alerts; the API must not report a successful *public* publication before the receipt. Retries use the same intent and generation, not a new staff authorization. A new manual action needs fresh authorization.

## 6. Proposed additive data and delivery design

**Private Auth/Admin database (approved placement; proposed ADM-03B SQL design):** create an unexposed `editorial_private` schema alongside, but separate from, `admin_private`. The proposed tables are `articles` (stable ID, slug, origin, head/live IDs, classification floor, generation), `revisions` (immutable snapshots, hash, number, parent, actor), `revision_contributors` (server-recorded authors/substantive editors), `review_events`, `approvals`, `approval_uses` (unique approval ID), `publication_intents` (unique request key and generation), `delivery_outbox` and `legacy_git_map`. Use FKs and uniqueness for article/revision pairing, `(article_id,revision_no)`, slug and generation; prohibit UPDATE/DELETE of revision/approval/audit evidence at the SQL boundary. Auth deletion must not cascade away history; privacy redaction/pseudonymization is a reviewed operational path. RLS is enabled and forced, with no `anon`, ordinary `authenticated`, `service_role` or broad `PUBLIC` table access. Executor grants are minimal and narrow functions use fixed search paths. A never-exposed private schema and explicit grants are both required; RLS alone is insufficient ([Supabase API guidance](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/api/securing-your-api)). No service-role credential enters frontend code.

| Proposed table | Contract-critical keys and constraints (not executable DDL) |
| --- | --- |
| `articles` | `article_id` UUID PK; `slug` unique and reserved; `origin` closed enum; `current_revision_id`, `published_revision_id`, `generation`, `classification_floor`; same-article composite FKs for both revision pointers; row lock serializes edits and intents. |
| `revisions` | `revision_id` UUID PK; `(article_id,revision_no)` unique; `parent_revision_id` same-article FK; immutable canonical snapshot, serialization version and server SHA-256; `created_by`/`created_at`; no caller-controlled approval or publication flag. |
| `revision_contributors`, `review_events` | `(article_id,revision_id,actor_id,contribution)` or event ID plus same-article FK; insert-only; independent approval checks the complete actor set, including inherited author. |
| `approvals`, `approval_uses` | Approval ID PK with same-article revision/hash and policy evidence; immutable decision. `approval_uses.approval_id` unique, linked to one publication intent. Only an eligible, unused approval of the current revision may be consumed. |
| `publication_intents`, `delivery_outbox` | `(article_id,generation)` unique and monotonically increasing; server request key unique for idempotent retry; action `publish`/`unpublish`/`cancel`, expected revision, audit event ID, immutable payload digest and delivery status. Outbox insert is mandatory in the same private transaction as the intent and audit; a revocation-triggered cancellation receives a later generation. |
| `legacy_git_map` | Unique `path` and slug, source hash, cutover state, approved timestamp and tombstone; no silent fallback after CMS unpublish. Import is not a staff approval. |

**Public-read database (approved separation; proposed ADM-03C delivery design):** add separate manual-editorial projection tables and bounded `web_api` readers, plus a dedicated delivery role/RPC. Do not insert Admin content through `pub_admin.news_apply_sync`, mutate `pub.news_entries` or reuse the Collector ingest role. One public-read transaction compares monotonic article generation, writes the validated revision projection and atomically changes or clears the live pointer. Article page, News archive, RSS and sitemap read that same committed pointer; cache invalidation and maximum stale time are explicit acceptance tests. The existing observation publisher and Collector Cron remain separate.

For the initial cutover, CMS-sensitive article/list/RSS/sitemap responses should bypass independent CDN/static caches until coordinated invalidation is demonstrated. The current RSS `s-maxage=900` cannot simply remain in force for a newly unpublished CMS item; either a version-coherent purge/ack protocol or a reviewed `no-store` policy must precede activation. The public delivery receipt is issued only after the committed public pointer is readable and the selected cache policy is effective.

The private database transaction can atomically contain authorization, revision/approval checks, protected state change, audit append and **outbox**, but cannot atomically commit a second database or CDN. The delivery worker reads only committed outbox intents and writes only the narrow projection RPC. It is idempotent by `(article_id,generation)`; older or conflicting generations fail closed, and acknowledgements are reconciled to the source intent. Before dispatch it must check that the original intent is still current and that its actor, policy, scoped grant, session and applicable approval have not been revoked; a failed check cancels the intent and audits the denial. A revocation and dispatch-claim must serialize in the private database. External audit-head anchoring, outage limits, retention and privacy remain the [ADM-01A audit gate](adm-01a-security-architecture-plan.md).

**Cross-database limit:** the authorization check and public-read commit cannot be one PostgreSQL transaction. A revoke can race *after* a dispatch claim but before the public commit. The recommended V1 operational contract is a fenced, ordered cancellation/unpublish generation with a measured maximum exposure window and alert; a revoke committed before claim must prevent dispatch. This is a **proposed compensation design**, not proof of immediate cross-database revocation. Security/data review and explicit owner acceptance of the measured window are required before public activation. If that residual window is unacceptable, the delivery architecture must be changed; a green private-SQL test cannot waive this gate.

**Migration order, with no migration file in this PR:** (1) private schema and constraints, deny all, backfill none; (2) narrow action wrappers and tests, still inactive; (3) isolated public projection and worker identity, no public reader cutover; (4) read-only parity/shadow projection and legacy inventory; (5) per-article activation after exact URL/feed/canonical comparison; (6) separately approved hosted staging and production. Every step is additive and reversible only while no protected content/intents exist; once present, preserve data and use a forward recovery or reviewed projection rollback. Do not drop historical revisions or audit to roll back application code.

## 7. Transactional and security invariants

1. Use one lock order for every private action: active policy and live Auth/staff/grant rows via the existing evaluator; session-revocation serialization; article row; current revision; approval/consumption; audit head/outbox. Acquire potentially blocking locks **before** the final database-clock checks. Recheck JWT expiry, live session, AAL2/TOTP freshness, policy version, staff/role/grant expiry and scope after waits. A revoke committed first denies the **private action**; an action holding those locks commits before the revoke. This ordering alone does not settle the later cross-database delivery race described above. No cached permission or JWT `iat` proves step-up.
2. Accept only server-derived identity and article/revision rows. Enforce expected `current_revision_id`, snapshot hash and article generation with row locks/CAS. A stale tab, simultaneous edits, approval of another revision, changed classification or a forged article ID cannot publish.
3. The protected private mutation, approval consumption, audit append and outbox row all commit or all roll back. An audit-head failure or outbox conflict fails the action. The public projection cannot expose a revision without its committed intent, and never reads private drafts, Account, Companion or Guildbook tables.
4. Approval and publication are separate permissions. A normal self-publication by a delegated Editor is valid only with an active article/collection-scoped `news.article.publish` grant; an Administrator has no implicit publish right. A highlighted publisher, including Owner, needs a separate eligible approver. Unpublish uses `news.article.unpublish`.
5. Public delivery is an explicit second-stage result. Projection failure leaves the previous public state intact and alerts. Reconciliation compares private intended generation with the public active generation; it never silently invents a successful publish. Old article URLs and RSS GUIDs remain stable through republish and rollback.
6. Test negatives in disposable PostgreSQL 16/17 and pinned GoTrue/PostgREST: ordinary/anonymous/forged/stale sessions, no grant, wrong environment/resource, expired grant after lock wait, MFA expiry, concurrent revoke/edit/publish, revision mismatch, approval replay/self-approval, classification downgrade, asset swap, duplicate request, audit/outbox failure, public generation reorder and Collector isolation. Preserve all existing Account/Companion/Guildbook and News Collector tests. Hosted-target behavior is a separate staging gate.

## 8. Compatibility and phased implementation

**News Collector:** `pub.news_entries.id`, `(source_key,source_entry_id)`, `slug`, `canonical_url`, `published_at`, `official_updated_at`, thread card grouping, suppression and `pub.news_post_versions` stay under Collector ownership. An upstream edit may add a source snapshot but cannot create/consume an Admin approval. Collector `featured`/`importance` remain curated fields; a manual CMS classification cannot be inferred from them. Keep `/news/entry/{slug}` and its RSS URL/GUID untouched. Test V1/V2 collector payloads, revision refresh and current read RPC output before and after any future additive public-read migration.

**Git editorial:** keep all existing files and `/news/{slug}` served by the Git loader initially. Inventory content hashes and public page/feed/sitemap metadata before any import. A legacy import is read-only and does not claim current staff authorship or retroactively require approval for already public bytes. Shadow render exact URLs, canonical, body, sample/noindex, images, dates and RSS GUID; switch one approved article at a time, with a reversible reader flag while the old Git bytes remain present. Do not silently change old `publishedAt` or create duplicate feed items. New edits after cutover follow the full current approval rules. An unpublish removes the CMS public pointer, and the legacy fallback must **not** resurrect the old Git article. A durable cutover/tombstone map controls that precedence.

**ADM-03B, after the separately authorized merges of #201 and #202:** branch from the resulting `main`. Implement additive, initially inactive private article, immutable revision, contributor, review, approval, single-use consumption, publication/unpublication intent, outbox and legacy mapping tables. Integrate the merged ADM-01B-5 boundary through narrow create-draft, edit-to-new-revision, submit, review, approve-highlighted, publish-intent and unpublish-intent operations. Normal content needs current validation and scoped publishing authorization, not a highlighted-content approval by default. Every protected operation checks live identity, session, AAL2/freshness, policy, scope, staff status, expiry, revision, classification and independence. The protected write, any approval consumption, audit and outbox commit or roll back together. Policy remains deny-all and delivery stays inactive; no public projection or worker is part of this slice.

Build the slice in reviewable order: (1) inert private schema, constraints, restrictive grants and rollback guard; (2) immutable revision and contributor writes; (3) review/approval and single-use evidence; (4) publish/unpublish intents with atomic audit/outbox; (5) focused security review and exact-HEAD CI. Use synthetic identities and disposable PostgreSQL 16/17 plus pinned GoTrue/PostgREST. Test concurrent edits, stale revisions, approval replay and self-approval, classification downgrade, scoped grants, permission revocation, expired session/TOTP freshness after lock waits, audit/outbox failures and duplicate intents. Run the existing Account, Companion and News Collector regressions and all ten mandatory checks. Correct confirmed findings and retest; record the AI-assisted review as technical evidence, not professional certification. Create one separate high-level Draft PR targeting `main` only after both prerequisites have merged and the branch starts from that `main`.

**ADM-03C:** implement the separate public projection, delivery worker, bounded readers, route/feed/sitemap integration, staging cache/rollback tests and measured compatibility cutover. Publish/unpublish remains off until full staged end-to-end evidence and separate owner production GO. No Admin UI is required to define this data contract.
