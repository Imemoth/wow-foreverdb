**Fifty-two hours. Five players. One finish line.** On November 7, 2026, a Horde party on the European Normal ruleset became the first to reach level 60 in World of Warcraft: Forever, and they did it together, almost to the same heartbeat.

This is a community milestone, not a Blizzard announcement. It was achieved by players and reported by the community press, and AzerDex is covering it because it is exactly the kind of moment a launch gets remembered for.

## A race that ended as a tie

When WoW: Forever opened, level 60 was a distant horizon. Roughly 52 hours later, a five-player group led by Jokerd Prime, which the community now calls Team Jokerd, crossed it. Reports agree on the shape of the result: the whole party reached the cap within a very short window of one another, and the nearest chasers were still in the mid-50s when the news broke.

Trackers disagree by a few minutes about whose level-up landed first, and that disagreement is the point. The group built its whole run around **nobody being left behind**, so the community treats this as one shared world first rather than a single winner and four supporting players. So do we.

## The roster

Three Mages, a Paladin and a Shaman: a party built around one shared goal, with all five names credited equally.

## The pull-and-detonate engine

The route to 60 was not a scenic one. It was a grinder's masterpiece built on a simple loop: **gather, group, detonate.**

The [Paladin](/guides/classes/paladin) did the gathering. Cloud Bells pulled large packs of enemies into a single tight cluster, the kind of pull most parties would avoid and a prepared one can farm. The three [Mages](/guides/classes/mage) then did what Mages do best, unloading **Arcane Explosion**, an instant area spell that strikes everything within 10 yards, into the crowd. Three casters hitting one mass of monsters means every kill lands almost at once, and every kill pays everyone.

Lmgd Mmr's Shaman rounded out the five. The result was a loop that rewarded repetition and punished hesitation: pull, cluster, detonate, repeat, across more than two days. It is the principle behind any good AoE grind, executed at a pace and consistency few groups could hold.

## Level 59: the 4% problem

Experience is generous to a group that kills together, but it is not generous to a group that wants to *finish* together. Team Jokerd wanted to reach 60 at the same moment, and that turned the closing stretch into a puzzle.

The hardest stretch, according to the coverage, was the **final 4% of level 59**. The logic is easy to see: when a party levels together, one stray kill can push a single player over the line early while a teammate is still short. So the team had to count, choosing which creatures to kill and when, to keep every experience bar moving in step.

That discipline is what separates this from a fast solo sprint. Reaching 60 in about 52 hours is an achievement. Reaching it in lockstep, with five characters across three classes, takes planning most players never need.

## Why it matters

A launch needs a first shared story, and the first level-cap race gave WoW: Forever one. It showed that organisation can count as much as raw hours, and that class synergy, not just class power, can win a race. A Paladin who gathers a crowd and three Mages who erase it is a classic fantasy played out for real.

It also shows there is more than one road to the cap. Since the party's finish, a solo player has reached 60 as well, according to Icy Veins, and the community is already exploring every route.

For anyone planning their own journey, the lesson is portable. Read the class pages for [Mage](/guides/classes/mage), [Paladin](/guides/classes/paladin) and [Shaman](/guides/classes/shaman), start with the [beginner's guide](/blog/wow-forever-beginners-guide-2026), and remember that the first group to the top won as a team.

## Congratulations, all five

To Jokerd Prime, Burning Speed, Vitochie Gofast, Cloud Bells and Lmgd Mmr: congratulations. You turned a launch weekend into a story, and you proved that the fastest way to the top of Azeroth can also be the most generous. Every player who logs in this week has a new benchmark to look up at, and a reminder that the best races are run in company.

See you at 60.
